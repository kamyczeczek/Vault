# D-001 — pick the comprehension meter

## Question

Which comprehension meter should gate the plain-language mechanism comparison
(always-on style rule vs few-shot vs post-hoc rewrite), judging by the
`00_web_*` literature now in the graph?

## Context

Chunk IDs (all six `00_web_*` corpus chunks, read 2026-10-02):

- `00_web_are_llm_generated_plain_language_summaries_t` — 150-participant
  crowdsourced PLS study: LLM summaries are *subjectively* indistinguishable
  from human ones, but human-written PLSs produce significantly better
  comprehension; "automated evaluation metrics fail to reflect human
  judgment"; calls for methods that "explicitly optimize for layperson
  comprehension."
- `00_web_factpico_factuality_evaluation_for_plain_lan` — factuality of PLS;
  existing factuality metrics "correlate poorly with expert judgments on the
  instance level"; the simplicity-vs-factuality tension is real.
- `00_web_plain_language_adaptations_of_biomedical_tex` — benchmarked
  Flesch-Kincaid, SMOG, SARI, BERTScore, G-Eval against 5-point Likert:
  **G-Eval (LLM-based) ranked approaches similarly to the qualitative
  metric**; surface readability indices did not track quality.
- `00_web_relay_personalized_llm_generated_plain_langu` — comprehension
  outcomes (not perceived quality) are the thing that moves; personalization
  raises bias/hallucination risk — a safety counter-metric.
- `00_web_enhancing_accessibility_of_medical_texts_thr` — PLA via prompting
  strategies, QLoRA, MoA; confirms mechanism candidates live in the prompt
  layer.
- `00_web_simplifymytext_an_llm_based_system_for_inclu` — system, no meter
  signal; included for target-audience customization framing.

## Resolution

RESOLVED — **two-tier meter, reader task primary:**

1. **Primary (gold standard): held-out lay-reader comprehension task.**
   Multiple-choice comprehension + recall items generated from the *source
   material* (answers known by construction — oracle-first, per the
   project's verification bar), administered to a non-author model cast as a
   lay reader. Design mirrors `00_web_are_llm_generated_plain_language_summaries_t`
   (objective comprehension/recall measures beat subjective ratings for
   detecting quality that readers actually miss).
   - **Known approximation, logged:** we substitute a lay-cast LLM for human
     crowdworkers; validity rests on the G-Eval correlation finding
     (`00_web_plain_language_adaptations_of_biomedical_tex`) that LLM-judged
     scores track human qualitative ratings. Any headline claim needs a
     periodic real-reader spot check (manual gate G3).
2. **Per-iteration proxy: G-Eval-style LLM rubric scoring** (simplicity,
   informativeness, coherence, faithfulness — the four scales the
   crowdsourced study used). Cheap, runs inside an iteration; must agree
   with tier 1's ranking or the proxy is discarded.
3. **Safety counter-metric: faithfulness/factuality (FactPICO-style)** —
   simplification must not move facts; a mechanism that improves the
   comprehension tier at the cost of faithfulness is a regression, per
   `00_web_factpico_factuality_evaluation_for_plain_lan`.
4. **Explicitly rejected as primary:** jargon density, mixed-language ratio,
   and any surface readability index (FK/SMOG). Two of six chunks show
   automated surface metrics do not reflect human judgment; one of three
   shows the best LLM-based judge correlates with humans. Surface metrics
   stay only as *diagnostics* to explain *why* a meter moved, never as the
   meter.

Consequences:

- The comparison (D-002) scores each mechanism on: tier-1 comprehension,
  tier-2 rubric agreement, tier-3 faithfulness. Winner = best tier-1 with
  tier-3 not regressed.
- Building tier-1 needs a question bank per eval text (answers known),
  which is the first build item of D-002.

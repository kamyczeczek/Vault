## 1. Question
Which provenance defects does the deterministic-fingerprint rule actually prevent, and which does it not?

## 5–6. Claims (jsonl)
c01 (prevented): Stale reflection digests silently passing check-time — fingerprints make corpus drift loud at gate time (re-stamp or fail).
c02 (prevented): Cross-platform byte divergence (CRLF vs LF) invalidating provenance on clean clones — incident 2026-09-21: fingerprint fc0231 vs ad2962 per checkout fixed via .gitattributes eol=lf; the fingerprint is what *detected* the defect class.
c03 (prevented): Orphaned evidence — a digest whose cited chunk IDs no longer resolve is quarantable (reflection/stale/ + repair ledger), because stamps carry the exact corpus state.
c04 (NOT prevented): Wrong-but-stable corpora — a fingerprint pins whatever state exists; a bad chunk that builds cleanly still stamps fine. Content truth is out of scope.
c05 (NOT prevented): Ingestion-time errors (double-ingested source, e.g. the 00_/01_ double-prefix pattern observed in this study, boilerplate titles like 01_new_markdown_file) — provenance records *what* changed, not *whether the change was sound*.
c06 (NOT prevented): Non-determinism *inside* the build (hash order, locale) — the fingerprint is only as deterministic as its computation; the CI gate must run on a clean clone to catch that (lesson from the manifest byte-vs-string sha incident).

## 7. Limiting evidence
c04/c05 are framed as absence claims ("the rule does not prevent X") — the corpus has no incident of a bad-but-stable state passing; they are structural inferences from the rule's scope, flagged as such.

## 8. Mechanisms
fingerprint = hash of corpus raw bytes → all derived artifacts (graph db, digests, candidate stamps) carry the hash → gates compare stored vs computed; mismatch ⇒ stale ⇒ re-stamp via guarded replan or quarantine.

## 9. Failure modes
- Re-stamp deadlock (additive ingestion invalidates everything at check-time) — mitigated by guarded replan verifying cited IDs first
- Fingerprint computed over local worktree bytes vs committed blobs (the CRLF class) — mitigated by .gitattributes + fresh-clone verification
- Treating a green stamp as content validation (c04) — the stamp is a drift detector, not a truth detector

## 10. Confidence
High on prevented defects (observed incidents); medium on the "not prevented" list (structural argument).

## 11. Proposed experiment
Negative-control suite: (i) flip one corpus byte, rebuild → stamp must change; (ii) clone on clean CI → computed == committed (catches worktree-local hash divergence); (iii) ingest a deliberately-wrong chunk → stamp passes (documents c04 boundary). All three logged in the repair ledger.
